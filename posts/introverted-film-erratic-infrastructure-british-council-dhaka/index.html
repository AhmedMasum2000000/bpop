Introverted Film and Erratic Infrastructure: Moving Image in the UK and Bangladesh

ইন্ট্রোভার্টেড ফিল্ম অ্যান্ড এরাটিক ইনফ্রাস্ট্রাকচার: যুক্তরাজ্য ও বাংলাদেশের মুভিং ইমেজের ইতিহাস

Where do the histories of artists' film in the UK and Bangladesh meet, and what happens when no institution exists to hold the work?

How did artists' film develop in the UK, and how does that story compare with moving image in Bangladesh? That's the question at the heart of Introverted Film and Erratic Infrastructure: Histories of Moving Image in the UK and Bangladesh, a lecture and discussion at the British Council Dhaka, and a rare public conversation about artists' film in Bangladesh.

Artists' film in Bangladesh and the UK, side by side

The session features Benjamin Cook (@bwrc00k) in conversation with Dr. Imran Firdaus (@imranfirdaus17). In an illustrated lecture, Cook will trace the history of artists' film in the UK, from the experimental and politically urgent practices of the 1960s and 70s. Dr. Firdaus will respond with a parallel genealogy of moving-image practice in Bangladesh, from film societies and cooperative practices to short-film movements, digital video and installation.

Together, they ask:

  • Where do these histories converge?
  • Where do they diverge?
  • What happens to artists' film when there is no institution built to hold it?

Part of Midnight's Third Child

The talk is part of Midnight's Third Child, a year-long research project organised by Benjamin Cook and Kehkasha Sabah. It's supported through the British Council's Connections Through Culture Grant, with sponsorship from the Durjoy Bangladesh Foundation.

The project takes its name from Naeem Mohaiemen's 2020 book, in which Mohaiemen describes Bangladesh's cultural identity as occupying a "third space" within the larger postcolonial narratives of South Asia.
