Rokte Joba: Anime Ontology and Bengali Post-Mysticism

Rokte Joba: অ্যানিমে অন্টোলজি ও বাঙালি পোস্ট-মিস্টিসিজম

পড়ুন

  • VIEW POST ON INSTAGRAM ↗
  • ← HOME

An artist's manga practice meets theology, iconography and the devotional image in an offsite showcase for the Symposium of Post-Mysticism.

Rokte Joba is, in the words of its announcement, "an ongoing image practice and philosophical investigation into the bengali anime ontology". B-POP Tags flagged it as an Artist Exposure pick, spotlighting @roktejoba and the artist behind the lecture, @aashnai__.

Bengali anime ontology at the Symposium of Post-Mysticism

For the symposium of post-mysticism at @solo___show, held 21st–23rd August, @aashnai__ presented rokte joba: anime ontology & bengali post mysticism, an offsite multimedia showcase and lecture. Attendance details are on the Solo Show symposium page.

From manga practice to the devotional image

Drawing from her manga practice, she shares how she constructs her pictorial world through theological, iconographic and affective traditions "whose social conditions no longer feel inhabitable". The talk moves through circulating images in "the post-liturgical land", enchanted modernities, and "how the devotional image sustains fractures".

It's a serious, research-led take on anime and manga as visual languages, rooted in Bengali image traditions rather than simply borrowing a style.

Who else is on the programme

The listing names a long roster of contributors alongside her, including Jamie Sutcliffe + Zoe Taylor, Meltdown Your Books, Elizabeth Gabrielle Lee, Jacob Canet-Gibson, ojitosdeemoji, denpa archivist, knightmaid, Yudi Wu, saffron-p and Carla Martin, among others. A further group is listed under September, including Phoenix Isla Kea, Chrysanthi, mirror_doll, archive_doll and Rahul Hendriksen.

That breadth says something about the event itself: a gathering of artists, writers and image-makers working on what happens to belief and imagery after older devotional traditions lose their hold.

The B-POP cover keeps it simple: "WE FOUND ART" in bold hot pink.
