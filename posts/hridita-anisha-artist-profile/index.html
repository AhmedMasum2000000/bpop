Hridita Anisha: The Artist Building Culture, Not Decorating It

হৃদিতা আনিশা: যিনি সংস্কৃতি সাজান না, গড়ে তোলেন

  • ← HOME

She's not decorating culture, she's building it. Hridita Anisha turns the simplest lines of childhood into questions of identity, abandonment and belonging.

Hridita Anisha isn't decorating culture. In B-POP Tags' words, "she's building it." Our profile from 18 September 2026 looked at how the artist (@hridita_anisha) takes "the simplest lines of childhood" and uses them to speak on a nation's deepest questions: identity, abandonment and belonging.

Hridita Anisha's art: simple lines, heavy questions

There's a deliberate tension in that approach. Lines we associate with childhood drawings usually signal innocence and play. Put to work on themes like abandonment, they cut the other way, and the plainness becomes part of the point. Questions about who belongs and who gets left behind are hard to dodge when they're drawn in a hand anyone can read.

From indie zines to live gallery walls

The caption maps out how wide her practice runs: "from indie zines to live interactive gallery walls right in front of your eyes." Zines are small, cheap and passed hand to hand. A live, interactive wall is public and happens in real time, in front of an audience. Working at both ends means the work reaches people in their pockets as well as in the gallery.

If you get the chance to see one of her live walls, hold on to the caption's phrase "right in front of your eyes." In that setting the making is part of the work, rather than something that happens out of sight in a studio. Follow her on Instagram to catch what she makes next.

The cover of our post leads with a headline in bold magenta, "WE FOUND ART", above a group photo of smiling children and adults.

Why B-POP calls her a pillar

We closed the caption with "this is what a true pillar looks like." It's the reason she sits in our art and fashion coverage: her work isn't decoration added to the culture afterwards, it's part of the structure.

For more artists rethinking identity and heritage, read about Samaha Subah and nakshi kantha and Lunith.Void's psychedelic culture in Dhaka. The Open to All exhibition at Alliance Française de Dhaka dealt with selfhood and unspoken narratives too. More is on the B-POP Tags home page.
